# Presenting to Decide: Facilitator Guide

**Workshop:** Home Credit Indonesia talent pool · 2 hours, in person
**Audience:** 12–16 high-potentials from sales, risk, collections, product, operations and finance
**Room:** projector, cabaret tables (groups of 3–4), one laptop per group
**Deck:** `Presenting-to-Decide-HCI-Workshop.pptx`. It has 34 main slides plus 3 handout pages, and the full facilitator script is in the speaker notes.

> All numbers in this workshop are **illustrative**. They are made up for practice and are **not actual Home Credit figures**. Every slide that shows data says this.

---

## 0. Preparation checklist

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 1 week before | Ask participants to bring one real presentation topic they must give within 30 days |
| 1 day before | Print: case cards A/B/C (one set per table), cheat sheet (1 each), feedback form (2 each), 30-day plan (1 each) |
| 1 day before | Put stock photos into `workshop/photos/` and rebuild (see section 8), or insert them by hand in PowerPoint |
| Day of | Per table: sticky notes in 2 colours, A3 sheets, markers, index cards, one laptop with PowerPoint |
| Day of | Put a timer on screen (phone mirrored, or an online countdown) |
| Day of | Brief one volunteer to read the "Director" line on slide 30 |

**Making participation safe (Indonesian context):** ask tables or pairs to share, not individuals. Give 30 seconds of silent thinking before you ask anything. When you walk the room and see good work, ask that pair whether they would share it ("Boleh share nanti?"). Thank people by name. Never put anyone's slide on the projector without asking them first.

## 2. Facilitator script

The full script is in the speaker notes of each slide. This is the condensed version.

**0–10 · Opening**
- *As people sit down:* "Write on your index card one presentation you must give in the next 30 days. That's what you'll practise today."
- *Slide 2:* Show it in silence for 10 seconds. "You're the Director of Risk with 30 seconds. Which slide do you act on?" Take a show of hands for A, then B. "Slide A is complete. Slide B is useful. Same data."
- *Slide 3:* "Three silent questions: what's your answer, why should I care, what do you need from me?" Ask: "Which one is usually missing in our management reviews?"
- *Slide 4:* Give the empowerment promise. Credit the sources in one breath.
- *Slide 5:* "Red is you working." Ground rules: there are no wrong answers; English is fine and so is a Bahasa word when one is missing; feedback is always "one thing that worked, one thing to try".
- **Transition:** "Every good presentation starts with the person you present to, not with slides."

**10–35 · Audience and one message**
- *Slide 7:* Know / Feel / Do. "If they can't approve it, your ask is to escalate."
- *Slide 8:* "We were trained to present like a school report. Leaders want the reverse." Cultural note: "In a decision meeting, saving the leader's time is the respectful choice."
- *Slide 9:* "Read only the top box and you know what I want." Ask: "Which reason would the Director challenge first?"
- *Slide 10:* Read the SCQA example aloud in under 30 seconds. "In decision meetings: say A first, then S-C-Q."
- *Slide 11 (Exercise 1):* Run it, debrief, then: "You have the top of your pyramid."

**35–55 · Storyline**
- *Slide 13:* Duarte's what is / what could be. "The audience is the hero; you are the mentor."
- *Slide 14:* "If nobody could disagree, it's a topic, not a point of view."
- *Slide 15:* Read the five titles slowly. "Did you understand the proposal without one chart?"
- *Slide 16:* Ask people to raise a hand for the row closest to their next presentation.
- *Slide 17 (Exercise 2):* Run it; the swap is the part that teaches, so don't cut it.

**65–85 · Design**
- *Slide 20:* "A topic title tells me what it's about. An action title tells me what to think."
- *Slide 21:* Quiz: "12 months of 30+ DPD?" (line) "Sales by 7 regions?" (sorted bar)
- *Slide 22:* "Where did your eye go? Red is our highlight colour. Use it for one thing."
- *Slides 23–25:* For each, ask "What changed?" If you're running late, show Makeover 1 in full and spend 30 seconds each on the other two.
- *Slide 26 (Exercise 3):* Run it.

**85–100 · Delivery and Q&A**
- *Slide 28:* Demo the same number twice, rushed and then with a 2-second pause. "Power posing has weak evidence. Preparation builds confidence."
- *Slide 29:* "'Thank you' wastes your most-watched slide."
- *Slide 30:* Role-play with the volunteer you briefed. "Respectful does not mean silent."
- *Slide 31 (Exercise 4):* Run it.

**100–120 · Practice and close**
- *Slide 32:* This block is protected: if anything else ran long, cut that instead. Sit in with each trio for one round.
- *Slide 33:* Trios read their commitments to each other, not to the whole room.
- *Slide 34:* "Your next presentation is your next practice round." Hand out A1–A3. End on this slide.

## 3. Exercises and case data

### Case cards (print one set per table; all data illustrative)

**Case A · Rising early delinquency in East Java (risk / collections)**
- 30+ DPD at month 3 (MOB3), East Java: Jan 4.1% · Feb 4.3% · Mar 4.8% · Apr 5.6% · May 6.4% · Jun 7.1%
- National average stays flat at about 4.2%
- New-merchant POS volume in East Java: +25% in H1
- 12 electronics stores onboarded in Q1 under a relaxed score cut-off account for 60% of early defaults in the region
- West Java pilot: a tighter cut-off for new merchants cut early defaults by about a third
- *Your audience:* Director of Risk, plus the Regional Sales Head (who will push back on lost volume)

**Case B · 1-tap payment reminder in the app (product / digital)**
- Many customers say they missed their first instalment because they forgot the due date (about 4 in 10 in a customer survey)
- 8-week pilot with about 20,000 customers in 3 cities: on-time payment up 3.5 pts vs. the control group
- Build cost: IDR 1.2 bn (including QA and security review)
- Estimated collection-cost saving: IDR 4.0 bn per year → payback in under 6 months
- Risk: some pilot users complained about too many notifications in week 2
- *Your audience:* Chief Digital Officer and CFO (who will ask about payback and the risk of being wrong)

**Case C · Sulawesi partner-channel sales drop (sales / partners)**
- POS loan volume in Sulawesi partner stores (IDR bn): Jan 52 · Feb 54 · Mar 55 · Apr 49 · May 46 · Jun 45, which is −18% from the March peak
- In late March a competitor launched 0% instalments at the two largest electronics chains in the region
- These two chains are about 40% of Sulawesi partner volume
- Option on the table: a co-funded 0% promotion for 3 months on selected products
- *Your audience:* Regional Head and the Head of Partnerships (who will ask "how much will it cost?")

### Exercise 1 · Find your one message (12 min, pairs)
1. Pick your own topic, or Case A, B or C (1 min).
2. Write the decision you want in one sentence, starting with a verb (3 min).
3. Draft SCQA, one line each (5 min).
4. Read it to your partner. They ask "So what?" until the answer is sharp (3 min).
**Output:** an index card with the answer sentence and SCQA.
**Coach for:** topics written where an answer should be ("Update on East Java" → "Pause 12 merchants").
**Sample answer (Case C):** S: Sulawesi partners were our fastest-growing POS channel in Q1. C: Sales fell 18% after a competitor launched 0% instalments at our two biggest chains. Q: How do we protect those partnerships without a price war? A: Co-fund a 3-month 0% offer on top-selling items only, and review it in month 2.

### Exercise 2 · 5-slide storyline + headline test (8 min, groups of 3–4)
1. Choose one topic per group (1 min).
2. Write 5 action titles on 5 sticky notes: slide 1 = the answer, slide 5 = the ask (4 min).
3. Swap with the next table. They read the titles only, with no explaining (2 min).
4. They mark each title ✓ clear / ? unclear / ✗ topic only; you fix every ✗ (1 min, then fixes over the break).
**Sample storyline (Case A):** 1) East Java early delinquency nearly doubled to 7.1% since January. 2) 12 merchants onboarded in Q1 drive 60% of it. 3) All 12 joined under the relaxed cut-off. 4) A tighter cut-off cut early defaults by a third in West Java. 5) We ask to pause these 12 for 60 days and tighten the cut-off.

### Exercise 3 · Slide makeover (10 min, groups of 3–4, one laptop each)
1. Pick slide 2 or 3 from your sticky-note storyline; its note becomes the action title.
2. Choose the chart from the message (compare / change / part / relationship / one number).
3. Delete, grey, highlight.
4. Swap with another group (3 min): does the title match what the chart shows within 5 seconds?
**Self-check:** full-sentence title · one message, one chart · only one thing in red · numbers labelled illustrative.

### Exercise 4 · Tough-question drill (8 min, trios)
Roles are Presenter, Director and Observer; rotate every 2.5 minutes. The Presenter gives a 30-second answer. The Director picks one card and asks it firmly. The Presenter answers with acknowledge → answer short → bridge back. The Observer gives one tip.
**Question cards:** What happens if we do nothing? · Why should I believe the pilot numbers? · What does it cost if you're wrong? · Who else supports this? · Can you do it with half the budget? · Give me the answer in one sentence.

### Practice round (17 min, trios)
3 rounds of 5 minutes (3-minute pitch + 2 minutes of feedback using the form), plus 2 minutes to set up. With 16 people, run five trios and one group of four, and shorten that group's pitches to 2.5 minutes.

## 5. Participant cheat sheet (handout A1)

**1 · Audience**
- [ ] I know who decides and what they can approve
- [ ] I wrote Know / Feel / Do for that person
- [ ] I know their likely push-back

**2 · Message**
- [ ] My answer fits in one sentence and starts with a verb
- [ ] I can say my SCQA in under 30 seconds
- [ ] Someone in the room could disagree with my Big Idea

**3 · Storyline**
- [ ] Every title is a full-sentence takeaway
- [ ] Reading only the titles tells the whole story
- [ ] Slide 1 = the answer; last slide = the ask

**4 · Slides and data**
- [ ] One message and one chart per slide
- [ ] Chart type matches the message
- [ ] Clutter removed; one red highlight; sources and units shown

**5 · Delivery**
- [ ] Rehearsed aloud 3 times, once timed
- [ ] Opening promise ready; pause after key numbers
- [ ] Last slide shows contributions, not "Thank you"

**6 · Q&A**
- [ ] Top 5 tough questions listed and answered
- [ ] Acknowledge → answer short → bridge back
- [ ] Backup data in the appendix; follow-ups promised with a time

## 7. 30-day practice plan (handout A3)

| Week | Focus | Do (about 20 min) | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Titles | Take your last monthly deck → rewrite every title as an action title → read the titles only | A colleague understands the story from the titles alone |
| 2 | Answer first | Write SCQA in 4 lines before your next meeting → open with the Answer → keep it under 30 seconds | Your manager knows your ask within the first minute |
| 3 | One chart | Pick one chart you use every month → delete, grey, highlight → move the old version to the appendix | The new chart is in the real monthly pack |
| 4 | Deliver | Rehearse a real presentation aloud 3 times → list 5 tough questions with A-A-B answers → have one peer score you with the form | You send your before/after slide to the facilitator |

**Facilitator follow-up:** send a reminder email on day 14. Collect before/after slides on day 30 and share the best three, with permission, with the talent pool.
