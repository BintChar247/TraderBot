# Get the Decision: Facilitator Guide

**Workshop:** Home Credit Indonesia talent pool · 2 hours, in person
**Audience:** 12–16 high-potentials from sales, risk, collections, product, operations and finance
**Room:** projector, cabaret tables (groups of 3–4), one laptop per table
**Deck:** `Get-the-Decision-HCI-Workshop.pptx`. It has 28 slides plus 3 handout pages, and the full script is in the speaker notes.

**Design of this version:** four moves instead of eight frameworks. Each move is taught in one or two big slides, then played as a game, then practised. Theory and source credits are in the speaker notes, not on the slides. About 79 of the 120 minutes are hands-on.

> All numbers are **illustrative**. They are made up for practice and are **not actual Home Credit figures**.

---

## 0. Preparation

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 1 week before | Ask participants to bring one real presentation topic due within 30 days |
| 1 day before | Print: case cards A/B/C (one set per table), cheat sheet (1 each), feedback form (2 each), 30-day plan (1 each) |
| 1 day before | **Voting cards:** one red and one white A6 card per person, used for every vote |
| Day of | Per table: sticky notes in 2 colours, markers, index cards, one laptop with PowerPoint |
| Day of | A countdown timer on screen; brief one volunteer to read the "Director" line on slide 24 |

**Making participation safe (Indonesian context):** votes with the cards and table races come before any individual speaking. Tables answer, not individuals. Give 30 seconds of silence before asking. While walking the room, ask pairs whether they would share ("Boleh share nanti?"). Never put anyone's slide on the projector without asking them first.

## 2. Script highlights (full script in the speaker notes)

- **Slide 2:** Say it slowly: "You've prepared for two weeks. The Director looks at the clock: 'I have 30 seconds. Go.'" Then pause for 3 seconds.
- **Slide 3:** "Red card for A, white for B. Three, two, one, vote!" Then: "Same data. A is complete. B is useful."
- **Slide 4:** "Three silent questions: What's your answer? Why should I care? What do you need from me?"
- **Slide 7:** "In a decision meeting, saving the leader's time *is* the polite thing."
- **Slide 8:** Read it in a monotone "meeting voice". After the guesses: "The point was hiding in sentence four. If the room has to guess, you haven't answered."
- **Slide 9:** Demo the East Java opening in under 30 seconds and time yourself out loud.
- **Slide 13:** "Make up the takeaway. All data is illustrative. What matters is the form."
- **Slide 18:** The answers are in the speaker notes. Bonus problem 6: "What's the decision?"
- **Slide 23:** Say "East Java bad loans are now 7.1%" twice: once rushed, once with a pause. Ask which one they remembered.
- **Slide 24:** "Respectful does not mean silent or vague."
- **Slide 28:** "Your next presentation is your next practice round." End there.

Sources, credited in the notes: Barbara Minto (Pyramid, SCQA) · Nancy Duarte (*Resonate*, what is vs. what could be) · Chris Anderson (HBR, "How to Give a Killer Presentation") · Patrick Winston (MIT, "How to Speak") · Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic (*Storytelling with Data*).

## 3. Games and exercises

| Activity | Format | Time | Win condition / output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🎮 Card vote (slide 3) | Everyone | 3 min | Room picks B and says why |
| 🎮 What's the point? (8) | Tables shout | 3 min | Someone names "pause 12 merchants" |
| ✋ 30-second opening (10) | Pairs, standing | 10 min | 4 blanks filled; passes the "So what?" test; under 30 seconds |
| 🎮 Fix the titles (13) | Table race, 60 s per title | 6 min | Best title wins the card vote |
| ✋ 5 sticky notes (15) | Groups of 3–4 + swap | 15 min | 5 titles; every ✗ fixed |
| 🎮 Spot 5 problems (18) | Tables take turns | 4 min | All 5 (+1 bonus) found |
| ✋ Slide makeover (21) | Groups, laptops | 10 min | Another table gets the message in 5 seconds |
| ✋🎮 Hot seat (25) | Trios, rotate every 3 min | 9 min | Every person handles one card with acknowledge–answer–bridge |
| ✋ Showtime (26) | Trios, 3 × 5 min | 17 min | Everyone pitches once and gets scored feedback |

**Spot-the-problem answers (slide 18):**
1. The title says nothing.
2. Rainbow colours, so there is no focus.
3. The legend makes your eyes jump back and forth.
4. Gridlines and two-decimal labels are clutter.
5. Three bars per region hide the one story, East Java.
6. Bonus: no decision is asked for.

### Case cards (print one set per table; all data illustrative)

**Case A · East Java bad loans rising (risk / collections)**
- 30+ DPD at month 3: Jan 4.1% · Feb 4.3% · Mar 4.8% · Apr 5.6% · May 6.4% · Jun 7.1%; the national average is flat at about 4.2%
- New-merchant POS volume up 25% in H1
- 12 stores onboarded in Q1 under a relaxed score cut-off cause 60% of early defaults in the region
- A tighter cut-off cut early defaults by about a third in a West Java pilot
- *Audience:* Director of Risk + Regional Sales Head (who will push back on lost volume)
- *Sample opening:* "Today, East Java new-merchant volume is up 25%. But early bad loans jumped from 4.1% to 7.1%. So the question is: how do we keep growth without buying bad risk? I recommend pausing 12 new merchants for 60 days."

**Case B · 1-tap payment reminder in the app (product / digital)**
- About 4 in 10 missed first instalments are due to forgotten due dates (customer survey)
- 8-week pilot, about 20,000 customers: on-time payment up 3.5 pts vs. the control group
- Build cost IDR 1.2 bn; estimated collection saving IDR 4.0 bn per year; payback under 6 months
- Risk: complaints about too many notifications in week 2
- *Audience:* Chief Digital Officer + CFO

**Case C · Sulawesi partner sales drop (sales / partners)**
- POS loan volume (IDR bn): Jan 52 · Feb 54 · Mar 55 · Apr 49 · May 46 · Jun 45 (−18% from the March peak)
- In late March a competitor launched 0% instalments at the two biggest electronics chains, which are about 40% of Sulawesi partner volume
- Option: a co-funded 0% offer for 3 months on top-selling items only
- *Audience:* Regional Head + Head of Partnerships

**Hot-seat question cards:** What if we do nothing? · Why should I believe the pilot? · What if you're wrong? · Who else supports this? · Can you do it for half the budget? · One sentence. What do you want?

## 5. Cheat sheet (handout A1)

**Before you build:** ☐ I know who decides and what they can approve ☐ I know what they must know, feel and do ☐ I know their likely push-back
**1 · Answer first:** ☐ My answer is one sentence starting with a verb ☐ Today / But / So / I recommend in 30 seconds ☐ Someone could disagree with it
**2 · Story in the titles:** ☐ Every title is a full-sentence takeaway ☐ Reading only the titles tells the whole story ☐ Slide 1 = the answer; last slide = the ask
**3 · One chart, one number:** ☐ One message and one chart per slide ☐ Chart type matches the message ☐ Delete, grey, highlight: one red thing only
**4 · Own the room:** ☐ Rehearsed aloud 3 times, once timed ☐ Opening promise ready; pause after key numbers ☐ Last slide shows what you contributed, not "Thank you"
**4 · Tough questions:** ☐ Top 5 tough questions listed and answered ☐ Acknowledge → answer short → bridge back ☐ Backup data in the appendix; follow-ups promised with a time

## 6. Peer feedback form (handout A2)

Score each criterion 1–4 (1 = not yet · 2 = partly · 3 = clearly · 4 = executive-ready):

| Criterion | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| Clear ask | I can repeat what they want in one sentence |
| Answer first | Recommendation in the first 30 seconds |
| Titles tell the story | Full-sentence titles; headline test passes |
| One number stands out | Right chart; one red highlight; no clutter |
| Calm under questions | Acknowledge → answer short → bridge back |

Then write: **one thing that worked** · **one thing to try next time**.

## 7. 30-day practice plan (handout A3)

| Week | Do (about 20 min) | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Titles | Rewrite the titles of your last monthly deck; read the titles only | A colleague gets the story from the titles alone |
| 2 · Answer first | Fill your 4 blanks before a meeting; open with "I recommend"; keep it under 30 seconds | Your manager knows your ask within the first minute |
| 3 · One chart | Delete, grey, highlight one chart you use every month | The new chart is in the real monthly pack |
| 4 · Deliver | Rehearse aloud 3×; list 5 tough questions; have a peer score you | You send your before/after slide to the facilitator |

Follow-up: send a reminder on day 14. Collect before/after slides on day 30 and share the best three, with permission.
